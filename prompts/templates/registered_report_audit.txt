Focus on:
- results-blind value: whether the question matters, and the paper would be informative and publishable, whatever the results, including a precisely estimated null; whether the manuscript states what each plausible result would teach
- analysis-plan specificity: whether primary and secondary outcomes, their construction and timing, the estimating equations, controls, fixed effects, clustering, outcome families and multiple-testing adjustment, attrition and non-compliance handling, and heterogeneity analyses each pin down one analysis, or leave choices open that could be made after seeing the data
- power: whether the minimum detectable effects rest on stated assumptions (intra-cluster correlation, take-up, attrition, outcome variance, covariate R-squared) that agree with the pilot, baseline, or take-up data the manuscript reports, and whether the effects it can detect are plausible and policy-relevant
- contingencies: whether the protocol says what will be done under low take-up, differential attrition, baseline imbalance, implementation failure, or changes to the sample, timeline, or partner, and how deviations will be reported
- feasibility: whether the sample, timeline, measurement plan, partner commitments, and ethics approvals make completion credible
- premature inference: whether baseline balance, take-up, pilot, or early implementation data are presented descriptively, or are interpreted as early evidence of effects or mechanisms
- registration consistency: whether a linked registry record or pre-analysis plan agrees with the manuscript on outcomes, arms, sample, and timeline; inspect a registry record only when the manuscript links or identifies it
